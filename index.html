Why a Levitt Pavilion

A place where everyone belongs

Free for all

Every concert is free and open to the public. Families, neighbors, and visitors of all ages and backgrounds share the same lawn and the same music, at no cost, season after season.

An anchor for Southwest Houston

The pavilion anchors the transformation of the former Shell Gasmer property, turning a long-dormant industrial site into a welcoming civic destination beside 291 acres of restored greenspace.

Backed by a proven model

Levitt venues in Denver, Bethlehem, Arlington, Los Angeles, and beyond have shown for decades how free music revitalizes public space. Houston joins a national network with a track record.

The site

From industrial ground to common ground

The pavilion will rise on the former Shell training site at Gasmer Road and Dryad Drive, a property whose steel derrick and industrial bones tell the story of Houston's energy history. Rather than erase that history, the project builds on it.

The site sits beside Willow Waterhole Greenway, 291 acres of lakes, trails, and prairie transformed over 25 years by the community from flood-control land into one of Houston's most loved greenspaces. The pavilion continues that transformation, one property over.

It works

The Levitt model has transformed cities like ours

Each year the national Levitt network presents more than 1,000 free concerts in over 100 communities. Former brownfields, neglected parks, and shuttered industrial sites now draw thousands of neighbors to the lawn every summer night.

Photo: Levitt Pavilion SteelStacks lawn, Bethlehem PA
(former Bethlehem Steel site)

Bethlehem, PA built its Levitt Pavilion at the base of retired blast furnaces. It is now the centerpiece of the SteelStacks arts campus.

Denver's Levitt Pavilion draws thousands to Ruby Hill Park for free concerts all summer long.

MusicFest proves the demand

We are already producing free live music at Willow Waterhole. MusicFest brings a full day of bands, food trucks, and community celebration to the greenway every fall, a preview of what 40 to 50 nights a year will feel like.
