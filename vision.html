The Vision

A stage for
Southwest Houston

A permanent home for free live music, rising on historic industrial ground beside 291 acres of restored greenway.

The place

Two transformations, one story

Twenty-five years ago, neighbors began turning 291 acres of flood-control land into Willow Waterhole Greenway: six lakes, miles of trails, restored prairie, and a community that shows up for its greenspace.

Next door sits the former Shell campus at Gasmer Road and Dryad Drive, where the engineers who powered Houston were trained and where a steel derrick still stands. The pavilion is the next chapter of the same story: land returned to the community, better than before.

Southwest Houston, inside the Beltway, along Brays Bayou.

The plan

What we are building

The pavilion

A permanent outdoor stage in an open lawn setting on approximately 8 acres, purpose-built for free concerts to national Levitt venue standards.

The lawn

No tickets and no gates. Blankets, lawn chairs, food trucks, and thousands of neighbors sharing the same music under the Houston sky, 40 to 50 nights a year.

The larger site

The pavilion will anchor the future transformation of the former Shell Gasmer site, with room for the surrounding park to grow around the music.

Photo: the historic oil derrick at the former Shell Gasmer site

The derrick stays.

The landmark

The derrick stays

Every great Levitt site keeps the soul of its place, and Houston has its own icon: the steel derrick that has stood over the Gasmer site for generations. It stays, the visual signature of the pavilion and a tribute to the people whose work built this city.

Our logo crowns the derrick with a star. The derrick does not have one yet, and we like it that way: a proposed crowning element of the restored derrick, subject to site design, and a promise for the site's future.

Beyond the music

What this means for Southwest Houston

The front door to Willow Waterhole

The pavilion delivers what the greenway never had: a visible main entrance, parking, restrooms, lighting, and a safe, welcoming place for everyone. These amenities serve daily park users year-round, not just concert nights.

A stage for the pipeline

Parker Elementary to Meyerland Middle to Westbury High is the only continuous arts education pathway of its kind in America, and Beyoncé got her early arts training at Parker. Today that pipeline ends without a public stage. The pavilion is the stage, with roughly 350,000 neighbors within five miles and planned connections that will bring even more of Houston within reach.
