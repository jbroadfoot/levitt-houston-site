The Vision

A stage for
Southwest Houston

A permanent home for free live music, rising on historic industrial ground beside 291 acres of restored greenway.

The place

Two transformations, one story

Twenty-five years ago, neighbors began turning 291 acres of flood-control land into Willow Waterhole Greenway: six lakes, miles of trails, restored prairie, and a community that shows up for its greenspace.

Next door sits the former Shell training campus at Gasmer Road and Dryad Drive. For decades it trained the engineers who powered Houston. Its steel derrick still stands, a landmark visible across the greenway. The Levitt Pavilion brings this property back to life as the next chapter of the same story: land returned to the community, better than before.

Willow Waterhole Greenway, the pavilion's front yard.

The plan

What we are building

The pavilion

A permanent outdoor stage in an open lawn setting, purpose-built for free concerts and designed to Levitt Foundation venue standards, on roughly 7 acres leased for 30 years at $1 per year.

The lawn

No tickets, no gates, no front row, no back row. Blankets, lawn chairs, food trucks, and thousands of neighbors under the Houston sky, 40 to 50 nights a year.

The larger site

The pavilion anchors a 17-acre site now in master planning by TIRZ 20 and its consultants, with future amenities that can grow around the music: gathering spaces, education, and connections to the greenway.

Early concept imagery. The final pavilion design will be shaped through the master planning process now underway.

The landmark

The derrick stays

Every great Levitt site keeps the soul of its place. Bethlehem built its pavilion beneath the blast furnaces of Bethlehem Steel. Houston has its own icon: the steel derrick that has stood over the Gasmer site for generations.

Lit and celebrated, the derrick becomes the visual signature of the pavilion, a beacon for the site and a tribute to the people whose work built this city. Our logo carries it forward: the derrick, the star of Texas, sound waves rising, and the water of Willow Waterhole below.

Beyond the music

What this means for Southwest Houston

Belonging

Free admission removes every barrier. A Levitt lawn is one of the few places where an entire city shares the same experience at the same time, together.

Vitality

Across the Levitt network, free concerts bring foot traffic, local vendors, and civic pride to areas that had been overlooked, lifting neighborhoods without pricing anyone out.

Momentum

The pavilion joins Willow Waterhole Greenway, the boardwalk, and 25 years of community investment. Together they make Southwest Houston a destination.
