Why a Levitt Pavilion

Four stories, one stage

Free for all, always

40 to 50 free professional concerts a year. No tickets and no gates. One lawn, one stage, and a whole city sharing the same music.

A landmark reborn

The derrick stays. A landmark of Houston’s energy story becomes the backdrop for the city’s next great gathering place.

The front door to Willow Waterhole

The pavilion gives 291 acres of beloved greenway what it never had: a visible entrance, parking, restrooms, lighting, and a safe, welcoming place for everyone.

A stage for the pipeline

The country's only continuous arts pathway, Parker Elementary to Meyerland Middle to Westbury High, finally gets its public stage, with roughly 350,000 neighbors within five miles.

The site

From industrial ground to common ground

The pavilion will anchor the future transformation of the former Shell Gasmer site, where a historic steel derrick still stands. The derrick stays: a landmark of Houston’s energy story, and the future backdrop of a place built for everyone.

Next door, neighbors spent 25 years turning flood-control land into Willow Waterhole Greenway: 291 acres of lakes, trails, and prairie. The pavilion continues that transformation, one property over.

It works

The Levitt model has transformed cities like ours

Across the country, former brownfields, neglected parks, and shuttered industrial sites now draw thousands of neighbors to Levitt lawns every summer night.

Photo: Levitt at the Falls lawn, Sioux Falls SD

Levitt at the Falls opened in Sioux Falls in 2019 after a community campaign much like ours, and quickly became the place the whole city gathers.

Denver's Levitt Pavilion draws thousands to Ruby Hill Park for free concerts all summer long.

Houston shows up for free live music
